# Product Positioning Playbook

Most product copy leads with features or technology. People buy outcomes. This playbook gets the
message onto the real value.

## 1. Find the real buy reason (the value hierarchy)
Ask: what does the customer actually want, emotionally and practically? Rank everything:
1. **Primary value** = the outcome they are paying for (the memory, the saved time, the closed deal).
   This is the hero message.
2. **Secondary benefits** = nice follow-ons (sharing, collaboration). Below the fold, not the headline.
3. **Enablers** = features and mechanics (tagging, dashboards, integrations). The "how," not the "why."
   Keep these out of the hero entirely.

Sanity check: imagine a study of why customers hire this. It is almost never "to use feature X." It is
the result feature X produces. Lead with the result.

## 2. The hero
- Headline = the primary value, in plain, evocative language. Contrast against the customer's current
  pain ("Relive the day, not the camera roll") lands harder than a feature list.
- Subhead = the transformation arc (before -> what the product does -> after) in one or two sentences.
- One clear primary CTA. Trust/privacy as a closer line, not a brag.
- Put "How it works" high on the page so the value gets concrete fast.

## 3. De-emphasize hype
- "AI" and similar buzzwords are fatigued and often overclaim. Use them only where they are **true and
  additive**, and even then lead with the outcome the tech produces, not the tech.
- Be honest about what is genuinely the differentiator vs a commodity capability. Over-claiming erodes
  trust and reads as generic.

## 4. Audience splits
- If you serve two audiences (e.g., a pro and their end customer), give each its own page with its own
  primary value. The pro buys "deliver more, less work"; the end customer buys "the keepsake." Do not
  blend them into one muddy message.

## 5. Voice and mechanics
- Concrete over abstract; specific nouns and numbers over adjectives.
- Cut hedging and filler. Active voice.
- No em-dashes (house style): use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses.

## Before / after
- Before (feature-led): "AI face tagging and a smart album builder organize your shoot."
- After (value-led): "Relive the day, not the camera roll." Subhead then explains the album outcome,
  and face-finding is mentioned as the quiet enabler.

## Checklist
- [ ] Wrote the value hierarchy (primary value, secondary benefits, enablers) before any copy
- [ ] Hero leads with the primary value, not a feature or buzzword
- [ ] Subhead shows the before/after transformation
- [ ] "How it works" placed high; one clear CTA; trust as a closer
- [ ] Hype words only where true; per-audience pages if there are two audiences
- [ ] No em-dashes
